01

Executive summary

▸

For most of software history, understanding code was a byproduct of writing it. AI weakened that link. Code now enters repositories that no human fully understood, and the engineer's posture is shifting from author to approver of work they did not write and did not fully read.

Working hypothesis (high conviction). This shift is likely to intensify as models improve: more code, more trust, proportionally less reading, so the gap between responsibility and direct understanding is likely to widen. A statement about behavior, not settled fact.

Argot's durable role is awareness at the moment a developer accepts responsibility for code they did not fully read. Not "catch the AI's mistakes." Awareness survives correct code.

Two engines: an acquisition engine of rare, memorable catches earns attention; a retention engine of frequent awareness is intended to build the habit. Operating model: audit installs you, check-on-accept keeps you.

Product requirement The "check-on-accept" auto-run is not a shipped default today (see §2, §6). Do not describe it as present.

Standing decision Posture: conviction on the foundation, options on the destination. Primary metric: audit-to-habit conversion, which is not directly measurable today without new opt-in instrumentation (§8).

03

The behavioral invariant

▸

Working hypothesis (high conviction). The foundation, held as a belief, not a proof.

Writing, understanding, and being responsible for code were welded together: you wrote it, so you understood it; reading moved understanding between people. AI weakened the weld. Producing code is near-free; understanding is still human-bound. So engineers increasingly ship code they have not fully read, often rationally.

Refined (was "the gap widens without bound"): the gap between responsibility and direct understanding is likely to widen as agent output grows faster than human review capacity. A tendency, not a law.

What this is notNot "AI makes mistakes." Mistake detection may improve or be commoditized. The durable need is awareness of what is being accepted, which persists even when the code is correct.
04

The problem Argot solves today

▸

A developer gets a plausible agent diff, skims it, accepts it, and puts their name on the commit, carrying a low unease and no efficient way to resolve it. Type checkers and linters answer "is this valid?" (increasingly handled in the agent). The unanswered question: "is this how we do things here, and did anything happen in this diff I would want to know before I sign it?" Argot answers that against the repository's own history and structure.

Standing decision Describe the job as awareness at the moment of acceptance, grounded in the repo. Not "AI code review" (implies a second model's opinion); not "style/voice checker" (understates it, anchors to the shrinking axis).

05

The product's two engines

▸

Standing decision (D3) Build and market two complementary engines; do not collapse them.

Acquisition (memorable, rare): weakened/disabled/deleted tests, bypassed/muted checks, a tampered locked rule, a spectacular catch, a historical audit. The ignition. Reality the integrity detectors, rule-tampered, and argot audit all ship.

Retention (frequent): foreign deps, reinvented helpers, misplaced code, drift, crossed boundaries. Intended to build the habit via a fast accept-time briefing. Reality the detectors ship; Requirement the accept-time auto-run does not (§6).

Working hypothesis (not a decision): the retention engine is where Argot's long-term value is created — it determines whether Argot becomes durable infrastructure rather than a curiosity; the acquisition engine is the ignition. Depends on user behavior; unproven. Minority view (recorded): an earlier analysis wanted the memorable catch to be the whole story; rejected because a once-per-hundred-diffs tool does not build a habit.

Standing decision (D4) Frame the retention engine as awareness, not defect detection, so its value survives better models.

06

The daily habit and the acceptance moment

▸

Standing decision (D2) Operating model: Audit installs you. Check-on-accept keeps you.

  • Install trigger: argot audit. Reality zero-setup, shareable, exits 0.
  • The habit: a seconds-long briefing at the moment the developer accepts an agent's change, showing only what deserves attention.
Product requirement — not current reality (P0-1)Argot does not run automatically at the acceptance moment today. The only automatic-on-install wiring is a pre-write foreign-import "ask" guardrail (Claude Code plugin only, fitted repos only). Commit-time check is manual, agent-chosen, or user-wired pre-commit. Do not write "Argot runs automatically before you accept." Write "the onboarding should make Argot run at the acceptance moment," or describe the real pre-write guardrail precisely.

Standing decision (D8) Pursue onboarding that wires the check into the acceptance moment (or the nearest reachable agent lifecycle event) with no manual step. A committed direction; a requirement not yet met.

Standing decision (D14) Signal quality is existential; no detector ships default-gating above a defined noise threshold. Evidence required measured accept-time noise across all default detectors.

07

Strategic positioning

▸

Standing decision (D6) Posture: conviction on the foundation, options on the destination.

The foundation (full conviction): clean low-noise signal; local-first; repository-grounded; seconds; portable repo-owned config; embeddability; integration into the accept flow (a requirement); a useful individual experience. Every future we currently find plausible depends on it.

The destination (held open): invest only in what the foundation needs; defer future-specific work until a gate is crossed (§14, §15).

Rejected assumption That Argot must become an organization-level governance platform, and that remaining a standalone tool would be failure. Influence and value capture differ (git stayed a tool; GitHub captured the value). Neither destination is inevitable.

Standing framing Argot is an open-source product. Its long-term form is deliberately left open, and how value might eventually be sustained or captured is intentionally outside the scope of this strategy — neither assumed nor rejected. This document answers why the product should exist, not how value should eventually be captured; that question is deferred until there is evidence. The strategy optimizes only for what makes an open-source tool matter: usefulness, adoption, habit, and trust. Remaining an independent open-source tool indefinitely is a fully successful outcome.

08

North Star and its measurability

▸

Standing decision (D5) Primary metric: audit-to-habit conversion — run argot audit → install → enable recurring checks → still trust and use it after 30 days. Stars and installs are secondary.

The measurement conflict (honest)Reality Argot has no telemetry and cannot observe steps 3–4. The North Star is a conceptual target, not directly measurable today, and D13 forbids default telemetry. Do not imply retention is observed. Do not resolve this by adding default telemetry.
  • Measurable today (proxies): npm/Action/plugin installs, release-asset downloads. Weak proxies for reach, not retention.
  • Only with opt-in instrumentation: local metrics shown to the user; opt-in anonymous usage; opt-in dismissal/false-alarm counts. Off by default, anonymous, local-first.
  • Qualitative research: voluntary surveys; user-research cohorts; interviews on whether the accept-time briefing is kept on.

Working hypothesis Dismissal and false-alarm rates lead retention. Evidence required a privacy-compatible, opt-in way to observe dismissals.

09

The trusted analytical core (definition)

▸

Authoritative analytical path

The logic that detects findings, computes scores, determines severity, decides rule violations, produces machine-readable evidence, and affects exit codes or enforcement.

Current reality This path is statistics (frequency tables + callee clustering) plus, for redundant/misplaced, a local, deterministic code-embedding encoder (jina-code via statically linked llama.cpp). The encoder turns code into vectors; it is not generative or opinion-forming. layering uses a dependency graph; integrity uses a tree-sitter test inventory. All local, all reproducible.

Standing decision (D12) The authoritative path must remain reproducible, inspectable, runnable without any generative LLM, stable for automation, and grounded in checkable evidence. "No LLM in the core" means no generative or opinion-forming model decides a finding, score, severity, or CI outcome. It does not forbid the existing deterministic encoder, which is part of the core.

Optional non-authoritative assistance

A future LLM may explain a finding, help with docs, summarize, onboard, suggest a remediation, or draft a custom rule for human review. It must never silently create a finding, alter a score/severity, decide CI pass/fail, replace evidence, or become mandatory for the core local workflow.

Test for a proposed featureIf it changes what is flagged, the score, the severity, or the exit code, it is authoritative and may not be an LLM. If it only explains, summarizes, or drafts-for-review, it may be an LLM — provided it is optional and non-authoritative.

Product principles

▸

Operational consequences of §10; where a principle restates a decision, the decision governs.

  1. Signal quality is existential (D14).
  2. Noise destroys the product; a dismissed finding is a withdrawal from trust.
  3. Seconds, not flow interruption.
  4. Attach to an existing behavior, not a new ritual (D8).
  5. Brief, do not scold.
  6. The individual local check stays free (D7).
  7. Cloud is never mandatory for the core (D13).
  8. Local-first and neutrality are non-retrofittable trust assets (D13).
  9. Configuration is portable and user-owned. Reality
  10. Stay embeddable (CLI/JSON/SARIF/hooks). JSON is stable-by-intent; a versioned schema is a P2 gap.
  11. No generative LLM in the authoritative core (D12).
  12. Do not reposition as "AI governance" prematurely (D6, D9).
  13. Build organization-facing features only after repeated, concrete inbound demand (D9, §15).

15

Evidence gates

▸

Until a gate is crossed and recorded in the decision log, default to strengthening the foundation.

F2 — Team platform teams commit config unprompted; multiple contributors inherit one setup; requests for shared policy or cross-repo visibility; requests for centralized records/reports; sustained shared reliance on a collaboration layer, distinct from the individual check.

F3 — Governance explicit requests for tamper evidence, attestations, or provenance; procurement/insurance/regulation asking for proof of AI-code controls; need to prove agents could not bypass rules; the same demand recurring across unrelated customers.

F1 — Stay a tool high individual love/retention; weak team coordination need; users value the accept-time briefing over centralization; platform additions reduce simplicity without lifting retention.

Reassess thesis audit-to-habit stays low after accept-time ships; users disable for noise; model-native alternatives remove most findings; the tool rarely finds anything actionable; the acceptance moment moves or disappears.

F5 — Pre-generation agents/IDEs ask to consume the convention model to write code up front.

Product roadmap principles

▸

Standing decision (D9) Nail the shared foundation; keep branch points cheap and open; exercise a branch only when its gate is crossed.

Transmission mechanism Reality, partial The config file committed to the repo carries Argot from one developer to a team (as ESLint/Prettier configs do). Note: one-click "codify a repeated finding as a rule" is a P2 gap (argot-suggest-rules is assisted authoring today); a durable local finding record is a P2 gap.

Illustrative sequencing, held loosely: win the accept-time briefing → let committed config spread to teams → become part of the definition of done in CI → only if gates cross, shared records or attestation. Prioritized gaps: ARGOT_PRODUCT_GAPS.md.

Distribution and onboarding principles

▸
  1. Front door: argot audit, zero-setup. Reality
  2. Activation goal is the habit, not the install (measurability caveat, §8).
  3. Requirement default install should make the check fire at the acceptance moment (P0-1). Today the automatic piece is a pre-write foreign-import guardrail (Claude Code). Do not overstate it.
  4. Developer-first, bottom-up; never abandon the individual developer.
  5. Zero cost to try: free, local, non-blocking.
  6. Distribution through embeddability.
  7. The committed config is the team on-ramp.

The "voice" metaphor (D10)

Decision: demote, do not delete. As positioning, "voice" describes the shrinking style axis and is aesthetic where the stakes are trust. But argot means a group's private vocabulary, so voice imagery has brand coherence and is distinctive. It may live in visual identity, the name's story, and the emotional register — never as the headline or the sentence explaining the product.

Current reality The shipped --help tagline and site still lead with "voice." Aligning them is out of scope here; recorded as positioning gap P0-3. Evidence required to reverse: research that "voice" is the phrase users actually repeat.

Risks and failure modes

▸
  • Noise kills the habit (largest risk). Mitigation: D14; watch dismissal/false-alarm (needs P1-3).
  • Accept-time integration never becomes automatic. Mitigation: P0-1 is the top gap.
  • Retention findings erode as models improve. Mitigation: D4. Open whether it fully holds.
  • Incumbent bundling. Partial defense: independence + the accept-time habit; bundled-good-enough wins low/mid stakes.
  • Verification demand erodes with trust (compiler analogy). Unresolved (§21).
  • Premature platform/governance investment. Mitigation: §15 gates.
  • Positioning drift back to "voice" or over-claiming unshipped features. Mitigation: §0, §19, reality doc.
  • Over-reliance on stars. Mitigation: D5 — while acknowledging the measurement gap (§8).
